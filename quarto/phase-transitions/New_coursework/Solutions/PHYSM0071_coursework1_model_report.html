Density distributions and finite-size effects

At (1.2T_c), (P_L()) is a single broad peak centred at (1/2). At (T_c), the distribution is broad and double peaked. Below (T_c), the two sector-conditioned peaks are narrow and lie near () and (0.95). The low-start run changed sector three times, whereas the high-start run did not. After applying (-) to the high-density distribution, its peak agrees with the low-density distribution within the sampling noise, as required by particle–hole symmetry. The residence times themselves are not reliable estimates of the equilibrium sector weights.

An equilibrium average (/2) below (T_c) would consequently describe an equal mixture of low- and high-density states, not typical homogeneous configurations at density (1/2). Individual configurations reside overwhelmingly in one phase; the mean lies between the peaks because it averages over phase identity.

Since \(U_4\) measures dimensionless distribution shape, its approximate independence of \(L\) is evidence of scale invariance. In real space this means that critical configurations have no characteristic domain size: density fluctuations occur over a broad range of scales, up to the system size. The uncertainty grows with (L) because the dynamics slows. For the extended (L=24) diagnostic run, the block-bootstrap uncertainty became stable as the block length increased. Both signs of (m) were sampled, with 1355 crossings, and the first- and second-half distributions differ by only 0.022 in total variation. Their mean densities, 0.440 and 0.449, also agree within the slow fluctuations. These checks support the reported even moments and distribution shape, although the relative sector weights remain less well determined.
