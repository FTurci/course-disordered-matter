25:# Part I – Theory Reminder and Experimental Design
26:
27:## Brownian motion versus diffusion
28:
29:Previous lectures introduced Brownian motion and diffusion.
30:
31:At the microscopic level, particles execute random trajectories due to collisions with solvent molecules.
32:
33:At the macroscopic level, this random motion generates the diffusion equation
34:
35:$$
36:\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}
37:=
38:D
39:\frac{\partial^2 \rho}{\partial x^2}.
40:$$
41:
42:Rather than following individual trajectories, we shall work with the concentration field
43:
44:$$
45:\rho(x,t).
46:$$
47:
48:The practical question for today is therefore:
49:
50:> How can we determine the diffusion coefficient experimentally?
51:
52:## What observable should we measure?
53:
54:For a localized initial condition,
55:
56:$$
57:\rho(x,0)\approx \delta(x),
58:$$
59:
60:the diffusion equation predicts a Gaussian profile
61:
62:$$
63:\rho(x,t)
64:=
65:\frac{N}{\sqrt{4\pi Dt}}
66:\exp
67:\left(
68:-\frac{x^2}{4Dt}
69:\right).
70:$$
71:
72:Instead of focusing on the profile itself, we characterize it using its Full Width at Half Maximum (FWHM).
73:
74:For a Gaussian distribution,
75:
76:$$
77:W
78:=
79:4\sqrt{\ln(2)\,Dt}.
80:$$
81:
82:Therefore
83:
84:$$
85:W^2
86:=
87:16 \ln(2)\,Dt.
88:$$
89:
90:The diffusion coefficient can be obtained directly from the growth of the profile width.
91:
92:::: {.callout-note}
93:Key idea:
94:
95:Measure the width of the particle cloud as a function of time.
96:
97:No particle tracking is required.
98::::

100:# Creating a Localized Initial Condition
101:
102:## Experimental challenge
103:
104:The diffusion equation assumes an initially localized particle distribution.
105:
106:How can we create such a state experimentally?
107:
108:## Superparamagnetic colloids
109:
110:The system used in this experiment consists of superparamagnetic colloidal particles suspended in water.
111:
112:When an external magnetic field is applied:
113:
114:- particles acquire induced dipole moments;
115:- dipoles align with the field;
116:- particles attract each other along the field direction;
117:- linear chains form spontaneously.
118:
119:**[Figure: Formation of chains under an applied magnetic field]**
120:
121:The chain provides an experimental realization of an initially narrow concentration profile.
122:
123:## Switching on diffusion
124:
125:The magnetic field is suddenly removed.
126:
127:The induced magnetic dipoles disappear.
128:
129:The chains become unstable and particles diffuse away from their initial positions.
130:
131:**[Figure: Sequence of experimental images showing chain disintegration]**
132:
133:The experiment therefore directly reproduces the localized initial condition discussed in diffusion theory.
134:
135:# From Images to Density Profiles
136:
137:The microscope does not measure density.
138:
139:It measures images.
140:
141:To connect experiment and theory we must transform images into concentration profiles.
142:
143:The analysis consists of four steps:
144:
145:1. Thresholding.
146:2. Binarization.
147:3. Profile extraction.
148:4. Gaussian fitting.
149:
150:## Step 1: Thresholding
151:
152:Particles appear darker than the background.
153:
154:We choose a threshold intensity.
155:
156:Pixels are classified as either:
157:
158:- particle;
159:- background.
160:
161:**[Figure: Raw image and threshold value]**
162:
163:## Step 2: Binarization
164:
165:After thresholding,
166:
167:$$
168:I(x,y)
169:=
170:\begin{cases}
171:1 & \text{particle}\\
172:0 & \text{background}.
173:\end{cases}
174:$$
175:
176:The resulting binary image contains only the information needed for particle detection.
177:
178:**[Figure: Binary image obtained after thresholding]**
179:
180:## Step 3: Averaging
181:
182:Since the chain is approximately vertical, we average over the vertical direction:
183:
184:$$
185:\rho(x)
186:=
187:\frac1{N_y}
188:\sum_y I(x,y).
189:$$
190:
191:This produces a one-dimensional concentration profile.
192:
193:**[Figure: Binary image and resulting concentration profile]**
194:
195:## Step 4: Gaussian fitting
196:
197:Diffusion predicts a Gaussian profile.
198:
199:We therefore fit
200:
201:$$
202:\rho(x)
203:=
204:A
205:\exp
206:\left[
207:-\frac{(x-x_0)^2}
208:{2\sigma^2}
209:\right].
210:$$
211:
212:The fitted parameter $\sigma$ provides the profile width.
213:
214:The corresponding FWHM is
215:
216:$$
217:W
218:=
219:2\sqrt{2\ln 2}\,\sigma.
220:$$
221:
222:At this point we have all the ingredients required to measure a diffusion coefficient.
